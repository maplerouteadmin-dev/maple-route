# Maple Route

A private, second-screen MapleStory Classic World route companion with turn-by-turn portal directions. **Live GPS** watches a shared game window, reads only the mini map corner to know which map you are on and where your character stands, and updates the route and spoken directions as you move. It never reads game memory, injects into the client, sends keys or clicks, or uploads anything.

**Starting it:** double-click the **Maple Route** desktop shortcut (create it once with `powershell -ExecutionPolicy Bypass -File scripts/create-shortcut.ps1`). It starts the app's own small local server when needed, so nothing else has to be running.

The app has six tabs: **Live GPS** (the front screen: only the live card and the map, plus a **Choose destination** link), **Go to** (pick a destination by map, monster/NPC, quest, item to farm or leveling guide, with directions, route style and taxi toggle), **World map** (explore any map's connections with monster levels, NPCs and rides; search and Go from there; plus the interactive portal map), **Quests** (quests for your level, what you have finished, then Quest Helper sync), **Hunt & trade** (live EXP tracker, sessions, market prices, sell check, gear), and **Settings** (live tools, map data, privacy).

## Use

1. Open the site in Chrome or Edge on a desktop. Under **Plan your route**, pick your destination (type to filter the map lists, or use the arrow for the full list). The app fetches public maps and portals from OSMS Data Explorer and caches them in the browser.
2. Press **Start live GPS** and share the MapleStory window once. About once a second the app samples a frame, finds the mini map window (top-left, else top-right) by its pale-blue title strip, and OCRs the "street / map" title only when it changes. When the map changes it advances or recalculates the route and, with **Voice** on, speaks the next step. The yellow player dot and the blue portal rings on the mini map are matched to the portal coordinates in the map data, which gives your position on the map: **Head right, about one screen, then climb up** or **You are at the portal. Press ↑.** Live GPS checks the screen 2.5 times a second. When the map title changes, the pictures of every connected map (preloaded as soon as you arrive) are compared with your mini map first, so the change is recognised before the title text is even read; a title read of a connected map is then accepted at once, while an unconnected map must be read twice. Status, Quest Helper and chat reading pause for 2.5 s after a map change. Stop at any time with **Stop live GPS** or the browser's sharing bar. Browsers never remember a screen share, so each start needs one pick (the page asks as soon as it opens). `scripts/create-shortcut.ps1` makes a **Maple Route** desktop shortcut that opens the app in its own Edge window with the picker answered automatically (`--auto-select-desktop-capture-source`, default "Screen 1"; it uses a separate Edge profile, so move progress over once with the backup buttons); choose **Entire screen** (with the game borderless or full screen) because a single-window share can end when the game rebuilds its window on a map change. If the share ends anyway, Live GPS pauses with a **Reconnect** button and keeps your route and EXP run.
3. **Map** shows the map's mini map art by default (it never shows monsters; tick **Detailed render** for OSMS's full render, which can), with every exit, NPC ride and NPC drawn at its exact coordinates (OSMS portal coordinates are pixels of that render), your live dot and a dashed line to the exit you need. Renders load on demand through the app's `/osms-data` relay. Your position comes from matching your in-game mini map against the map's mini map picture; that picture is lined up with the render automatically (exact for every map, no visit needed) and live portal rings refine it. Coming through a known portal places you at its arrival spot straight away. If the render is unavailable, the mini map picture or a coordinate drawing is used instead. Click an exit to make its map your destination. **When you reach your destination** the Live card switches to **You're here**: where you stand on the map, every monster with level fit, spawns, EXP, HP and community-recorded drops, NPCs and the quests they give or take (closest to your level first), exits and rides as one-click routes, the level range the map suits, your best logged run there and active quest steps on this map.
4. **Live tools** (under Settings) use the same shared window:
   - **EXP tracker** reads "EXP value [percent]" and your level every few seconds, rejects readings that disagree with the EXP bar's fill, and shows EXP/hour, %/hour and time to the next level under **Hunt & trade → Live EXP tracker**. Level-ups and EXP lost on death are handled. **Save as training session** adds the run to **My training sessions** (mesos are not on the status bar, so they are not tracked).
   - **Quest Helper sync** reads every quest in the in-game Quest Helper (blue or see-through, found by its AUTO button and border), matches its (possibly truncated) title to the wiki quest catalog, adds the quest to your active quests, ticks finished counts, and when every count is done says who to hand it in to, with a route there.
   - **Guide me** (Quests → Quest Helper sync, or the Live card) follows one quest by its live counts: it routes you to the map that best covers the unfinished counts (monsters to defeat, and monsters the wiki records as dropping the items), says **Hunt here** with progress bars and keeps you there until the counts are done, then routes you to the hand-in NPC; when the quest leaves the Quest Helper it moves on to your next tracked quest.
   - **Trade chat prices** reads the last chat lines and logs `S>`/`B>` adverts that name an item and a price. **Hunt & trade → Market prices** shows the median asking and buying price per item with ranges and counts. These are adverts, not completed sales, and are stored only in this browser.
   - **Shop or players?** (Hunt & trade) compares what an NPC shop pays for an item (the game's item value from the OSMS catalog) with the player price from your trade-chat log or a price you type in, takes 15% off asking prices, and says whether to sell to a shop or list it. **Worth listing for players** ranks logged items whose player price clearly beats the shop. It links to NiaMeowDB's Price Check for current listings; NiaMeowDB data is not fetched automatically because its reuse policy asks for permission first.
5. **Pop-out window** opens a small always-on-top window laid out like the in-game mini map (map name on top, the map with your dot, the next move below) so it can sit over the game's mini map. Its **World map** button switches it to the world map (and enlarges it) to search and pick a new destination without leaving the game; **Back** returns to the mini map. A faint grey strip in its bottom-left corner shows a tiny picture of each monster that spawns on the map with its spawn count (from OSMS data, not read off the screen; turn it off under Settings → Monster count in pop-out). Browsers don't let pages place it, so drag it to the corner once (Document Picture-in-Picture, Chrome/Edge 116+).
6. Without Live GPS, pick your current map and press **I'm on [next map]** after each portal, or use **Check current screen** / **Use a screenshot file** for a one-frame check. If the automatic reader misses, you can mark the title on that frame. **Speak next step** reads the upcoming step on demand.
7. **Quest guide & directions** searches the MapleClassic Wiki quest catalog and loads structured steps for the quest you choose. It matches named NPCs, monsters, and maps to the route feed; choose a location to get portal directions. Collection steps without a known map lead to the item finder. Tick steps yourself; progress stays in this browser. **Quest stops** remains available for your own errands.
8. **Find a mob or NPC** searches the public location listings and routes to a selected map, not to a precise position within it.
9. **Level guide** ranks training candidates using monster level, listed spawn count, EXP and HP. Money candidates use spawn density only; the source does not supply verified drop rates, item prices, or meso-per-hour figures. Treat these as places to investigate, not guaranteed returns.
10. **Find an item to farm** searches the OSMS item catalog. After choosing an item, the browser asks MapleClassic Wiki for community-recorded dropping monsters, joins them to OSMS monster-spawn maps, and offers route choices ranked by listed spawns and portal distance. One recorded monster is **not** proof of an exclusive drop; records and drop rates may be incomplete.
11. **Classic leveling journey** gives a short 1–55 progression with map alternatives, hazards, and route buttons. It highlights the bracket for your entered level. Above 55, use the live candidate finder rather than treating an outdated static guide as current.
12. **Interactive portal map** shows a selected map and up to eight direct neighbors in a schematic diagram. Click a neighbor to explore, search any map by name, or use the focused map as your start or destination. Green lines are outgoing portals; dashed amber links only arrive at the center. The diagram is not geographic game terrain.
13. **Next stop** stores a manually entered level, job, and focus (active quests, training, money candidate, or chosen item) in the browser. It offers one map and a directions button when the current map is known. Training and money suggestions are estimates from the existing feeds, not measured returns.
14. **Quest trip planner** accepts up to eight active quests from the quest guide. It takes the next unchecked step of each quest, groups steps sharing a map, and proposes a practical portal order from the current map. Marking a step complete unlocks the following step. The order is a bounded heuristic, not a globally shortest trip. Community required-item rows become manual "in my bag" checks; level and job mismatch are warnings, not automatic eligibility decisions. Check the full quest page for prerequisites that are not structured in the feed.
15. **Gear & crafting planner** uses the chosen item to compare documented monster drops, NPC shop listings, and a small set of explicitly sourced starter recipes. Enter how many of each ingredient you have to see what remains, then jump to the item finder for missing materials. Route buttons appear only when a named shop or crafting master matches a map or NPC in the current route feed. Recipe data is marked pre-launch by the community source and must be verified in the game before spending resources.
16. **Route style** offers fewest portals, an option that penalizes travel through maps with listed monsters much higher than your saved level, and an option that penalizes exits whose labels or source costs suggest special travel. These modes rank the same directed portal graph; none proves that an exit is safe, ungated, or presently accessible. The route explains its tradeoff and notes flagged exits and higher-level maps.
17. **Party-quest checklist** covers the community-documented launch party quest *First Time Together*: preparation, stage-by-stage manual checks, saved progress, and a route to Lakelis when mapped. The walkthrough is marked pre-launch; confirm eligibility, entry channels, and mechanics in the game. No party status or instance progress is read.
18. **Progress backup** downloads a JSON file containing your map choice, quest checks, goals, gear-material counts, party checks, and recorded sessions. Restore that file from the data section on another browser or after clearing site data. Restoring replaces the personal progress in that browser after confirmation; it does not replace the public map cache. Map data and personal progress are stored under separate browser keys so a large map cache cannot block saving personal progress.
19. **My training sessions** accepts a map, elapsed minutes, starting and ending levels, EXP, and mesos. For a level-up, enter total EXP gained manually because the current-level EXP counter resets. It calculates your observed per-hour rates, keeps up to 100 sessions locally, and uses a nearby-level logged run for the dashboard's training or money focus when available. A logged rate is not a promise of future performance.

Screen sharing requires HTTPS or localhost and a browser that supports `getDisplayMedia`. The game may need to be in windowed or borderless mode to appear in the picker. The mini map must be open and show its title. Sampling runs from a worker timer so it keeps working while the game, not this tab, is in front. Frames never leave the browser and are discarded after each sample. The reader was tuned on Classic World screenshots at 2000×1120; other UI scales should work because sizes are measured relative to the mini map's title strip, but report misses with a screenshot.

## Data and sources

- Public maps, portals, NPC locations and monsters are fetched directly from [OSMS Data Explorer](https://osmsdataexplorer.com/) in each visitor's browser, at most once a day unless the player refreshes them. The project does not bundle or republish its dataset. Feed formats are validated before data replaces a working local copy.
- Item names are fetched from OSMS on demand. Drop relations are queried per selected item from [MapleClassic Wiki](https://mapleclassic.wiki/) and credited to its contributors under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/), subject to the wiki's "unless otherwise noted" caveat. The app joins these records to OSMS spawns and routes; no item catalog or drop table is bundled.
- Quest names and step targets are queried on demand from MapleClassic Wiki's public Cargo API, credited to its contributors under the same license. Quest prerequisites and steps may be incomplete; open the linked full guide before relying on a route. No quest data is bundled and no in-game quest log is read.
- Shop listings are queried per chosen item from MapleClassic Wiki's public Cargo API. The small starter recipe selection is adapted from its Smithing, Weaponcrafting, and Tailoring pages under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0; it is incomplete and labeled pre-launch. Material counts are entered by the player and kept only in browser storage. The planner never reads inventory or assumes an item has only the listed sources.
- The short leveling-journey summaries are adapted from the [MapleClassic Wiki training guide](https://mapleclassic.wiki/wiki/Training_guide), credited to its contributors under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). The suggestions are not guaranteed best for a class, character, or later game version.
- Taxi destinations and fares (`src/transport.js`) are facts taken from the [Regular Cab](https://mapleclassic.wiki/wiki/Regular_Cab), per-town cab, [Phil](https://mapleclassic.wiki/wiki/Phil) and [VIP Cab](https://mapleclassic.wiki/wiki/VIP_Cab) pages of MapleClassic Wiki (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0).
- The party-quest checklist is adapted from the [First Time Together](https://mapleclassic.wiki/wiki/First_Time_Together) walkthrough under CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. The wiki marks the walkthrough pre-launch; no claim is made that every detail is verified in the current client.
- [NiaMeowDB's Pathfinder](https://meowdb.com/msclassic/ko/pathfinder) and [quest pages](https://meowdb.com/msclassic/ko/quests) are linked as references. Their catalog is not copied. Its [reuse policy](https://meowdb.com/reuse) requires permission for wholesale reuse.
- Map graphics, sprites, and game screenshots are not bundled. The app has no Nexon affiliation or approval.

The portal graph keeps each portal's kind from the feed: visible swirls (press ↑), walk-in exits, hidden portals (no swirl, often quest-gated) and scripted exits. Hidden and scripted exits cost more, so routes use them only when no visible path exists, and their steps say so. NPC rides come from the MapleClassic Wiki's transport pages: the Regular Cab in Henesys, Perion, Kerning City, Sleepywood and Ellinia, Phil's one-way rides from Lith Harbor, and the VIP Cab to Ant Tunnel Park. Steps name the driver, its side of town (from the OSMS NPC position) and the fare for your saved level. Ellinia's cab fares mirror the published reverse fares because its page has no table. Untick **Use taxis and NPC rides** to walk everywhere. Each step also says which side of the next map you arrive on, from the feed's arrival portal. Maps that are reached only through NPCs (subways, boarding areas, jump quests) have no portal path; the app says so and offers the closest reachable map. Routes do not prove that an exit is open to your character; quest gates and patches may change them.

## Authorized data import

Use **Import authorized JSON** to provide a map and portal graph you have the right to use. Format:

```json
{
  "schemaVersion": 1,
  "label": "My authorized map graph",
  "source": "https://example.org/source",
  "updated": "2026-10-21",
  "maps": [
    { "id": "map-a", "name": "Starting map", "area": "Region", "aliases": ["Alternate title"] },
    { "id": "map-b", "name": "Next map", "area": "Region" }
  ],
  "portals": [
    { "from": "map-a", "to": "map-b", "name": "east exit", "kind": "visible", "cost": 1, "note": "Optional description" }
  ]
}
```

Edges are directed. Include the reverse edge separately if travel works both ways. A `cost` above 1 can deprioritize an exit. Optional `kind` is `visible`, `walk`, `hidden`, `special` or `taxi`. The file stays in the browser's local storage where space permits.

## Develop

Run `npm install`, `npm test`, `npm run build`, and `npm run dev`. `vite.config.js` relays `/osms-img/*` to OSMS's `data/current/*` and `/osms-data/*` to `data/*` for `dev` and `preview`; a static host needs the same reverse proxy for the map renders and picture matching (everything else works without it). Production output is in `dist/`. The build copies a bundled English Tesseract OCR worker, WebAssembly core, and language model into the static site. No screenshot is sent to an OCR service.
